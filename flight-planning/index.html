Aerodrome Performance — Per Movement

Each departure and arrival is calculated with its own leg's wind. Takeoff/landing distance and climb rate use the reference-POH sea-level figures corrected by a standard +10%/1,000 ft density-altitude rule of thumb (−10%/1,000 ft for climb rate) — an approximation, not the actual airframe-379 altitude chart. Runway choice picks whichever fixed direction gives the lower crosswind component for that movement's wind, except Saguramo and Mestia, which are fixed one-way fields regardless of wind (Saguramo: land RWY09 upslope, depart RWY27 downslope; Mestia: land RWY02 upslope, depart RWY20 downslope). Both are gravity-favorable by design — this tool shows the slope but takes no distance credit for it, so real usable margin may be better than shown. Saguramo's runway dimensions and elevation are provisional/unsurveyed pending GCAA certification — treat with extra caution. Runway lengths and ATS frequencies below are fixed AIP or operator reference data — only the selected direction and the DA-corrected numbers respond to your inputs.

Planning tool only, generic-exercise figures throughout — not a substitute for the current AIP, NOTAMs, actual METAR/TAF, or the airframe's own POH before an actual flight. Route 3's waypoints (Sakadagiano, Agara, BT/ALI) and leg geometry come from an AirNavigation-generated navigation log (uploaded 24 Sep 2026); course/distance were converted from that log's NM/magnetic-course figures into this tool's km/course convention, and all time/fuel/climb numbers were recomputed using this project's own aircraft performance model rather than the log's generic placeholder figures (110 kt IAS, 41.7 L/h). Route 3 crosses notably higher terrain than Routes 1–2 — the source log calls for a transit altitude up to 11,200 ft between BT/ALI and Ambrolauri, well above this tool's simplified per-leg climb model (which uses 7,100 ft at BT/ALI and cannot represent a climb-then-descend "hump" within one leg). At this airframe's modest climb rate, reaching 11,200 ft in the distance available is marginal at best — verify actual climb performance and current MSA/terrain charts before relying on this routing; do not treat the tool's Route 3 numbers as a certified terrain-clearance profile.
